# FINDINGS — what the buried layer actually says

Each item: the rule, where it comes from, and what it buys you. Folio/page references are from the
files in `corpus/raw/`, so you can check every one.

## 1.

The *Libro de los juysios de calatarama* devotes a whole chapter (ch.2, ff. 4v-5v) to checking your
own work **after** the cast:

> if one or two of the figures associated with the planet of the day on which the casting was done
> appears in the judgement, and they are in the strongest houses as well as looking to the Sun and
> the Moon, this is indicative of the judgement being correctly done. If the judgement were cast at
> night, look only to the figures of the Moon. **If the figures of the day or night are not found in
> the casting at all, it is entirely useless.** The same with the figures of the hour.

Consequence: before interpreting anything, look up the day-lord's figures (f.5 table) and see whether
they occur, and where. In `engine/elections.py`. The hours are **temporal, not clock hours**
(the same source: they are "only of sixty minutes in length twice a year, at the equinoxes"), so
`planetary_hour()` divides the real sunrise→sunset span by 12. Using clock hours silently reads the
wrong lord.

**Buy:** a hard rule for "should I answer this cast at all", and a defensible reason to re-cast.

## 2.

From Cattan (1591 and 1608 independently — I recovered the same ruling from both, which is why I
trust it despite the blackletter OCR):

> If this figure Cauda Draconis be found in the first house, then the figure should not be judged,
> but it must be broken and an other made **one houre after that**.

Plus the pair from the practical tradition: **Rubeus** as first mother → the querent is lying about
the matter; **Populus** as Judge → too much is moving, no verdict to extract.

**Buy:** three concrete "do not proceed" conditions, one with a prescription (wait an hour).

## 3.

- *Calatarama*/Arabic (van Binsbergen's translation of al-Zanātī): they formed a new configuration
  called **'sand ruler'** … *"It is the fifteenth house. Then they have formed **from the first and the
  fifteenth house** a new configuration called 'the end result'. It is the sixteenth."*
- Digital Ambler (shield construction): *the Sentence … generated by addition from the Judge and the
  First Mother*.
- Britannica: the 16th is a *"super judge, or reconciler"*.
- Serena Powers / Lenz-line practice: *Reconciler = Judge + First Mother*; a **rarer** variant uses
  the first witness.

Names in circulation: Reconciler, Sentence, Superjudge (*superiudex*), Subjudge (*subiudex*), Fate,
Judge of the Judge, Result of the Result, al-ākhir / "the end result". **There is no standard
"C" name** — if your source calls it something on a C, it is most likely *Conclusion* or *Cauda*-
flavoured local usage, and I have not found a primary source that uses one. I checked.

And the popular shortcut is not a shortcut, it is a tautology: because figure-addition is XOR with
Populus as identity, **Judge + Left witness ≡ Right witness, always**. I proved it over the entire
space (65,536 witness pairs, 0 exceptions) and over every cast (0 exceptions). So the 16th figure
computed that way contains no information at all. `I + XV` is the only version that can say something
new.

## 4.

> if the bad figures engender other bad ones they demonstrate evil from two parts, and if the good
> engender good it demonstrates good from two parts (f. 22v)

and the manual then uses it diagnostically: Amissio in the 6th **kills** when it is born of
Fortuna Minor + Conjunctio, or Cauda + Rubeus, or Puer + Tristitia (f. 37); Conjunctio born of
Populus + Conjunctio "demonstrates the perfection of the union" in a marriage question (f. 42).

**Buy:** the same figure in the same house means different things depending on which two figures made
it. This is a layer almost no modern book mentions, and it is free — the parents are already in the cast.

## 5.

Figures repeat; a repeating figure "passes". The rule most books skip is in ch.5:

> look at the figure first, where it returns again, and twice, and make a mark on it —
> **namely the second, not the first** (f. 11v)

i.e. when a figure appears in two houses, you judge it by its **second** coming. Combined with the
entry/exit axis (mobile = outgoing/exiting, stable = incoming/entering), this is the "movement"
calculator: *where the matter is going, and whether it stays.*

## 6.

**Via Puncti** (head-line only): from the Judge's top row upward, following whichever parent shares
that row. Where it lands is the root of the question. Two refinements the French sources add:
at the Witnesses/Nieces it may **branch** — "two-headed or four-headed" — meaning several roots; and
if it cannot start, *"the situation is exactly as it seems"*. The Arabic version (van Binsbergen /
Olomi) is stricter and I follow it in the engine: only a Judge with a **single** head can form a clean
path — and the enumeration shows why: 32,768/32,768 of those casts reach a root figure without
branching. A Judge with a double head either branches or dies.

**Projection of the Points** (count the *single* dots in houses I-XII, mod 12 → house) = where the
hidden factor sits. **Part of Fortune** (count *all* points, mod 12) = where the gain stands;
remainder 0 counts as the 12th.

## 7.

Unit, from the figure in the house of the question (ch.6, f.12):
- **years**: Caput Draconis, Fortuna Major, Tristitia, Carcer, Albus, Laetitia
- **months**: Rubeus, Puella, Puer, Fortuna Minor
- **days**: Via, Amissio, Cauda Draconis
- **hours**: Conjunctio

Count, two ways:
- **A**: total points of the whole cast, digits summed once — their example: *"all the points in the
  judgement are 84, we will say twelve days or weeks or months"* because 8+4=12.
- **B**: points of the figure in the question house — *"if Via, which is made up of four points, is
  found there, he will be released in four days."*

Pace, by planet: Saturn/Jupiter prolong; Venus middling; Mercury short (Conjunctio shorter than
Populus); Moon shortest (Via shorter than Albus).

## 8.

For each question house, which triplicity and which **extra houses** to read. This is the answer to
"how do I check for the best alternative": the manual does not let you read one house, it hands you
the set. Examples: house I → 1-2-9, 5-6-9, 7-8-12; house II (money) → 1-2-9, 5-6-11, **13-14-15**
("13 is the witness of money"); house III → also read 8, 10, 14; house IV → 3-4-10, 11-12-14 and
9, 13; house XII → 1, 12, 6, 8, 7, 14. Note 13/14/15 (witnesses and Judge) are folded into the
routing — a medieval antecedent of what modern practitioners call the court.

## 9.

Find the house of the question → find the **other** houses that bear on it → read the figures in the
houses **neighbouring** the question house → identify the figures that rule the topic anywhere in the
cast (for money: Acquisitio and Amissio, because "Acquisitio is especially money… rich men, money and
poverty", "Amissio: loss of money, thieves and highwaymen") → see where the question's figure reappears
(good houses = accomplished with little effort; bad = effort and delay) → and so on to the witnesses,
Judge, and the "figure of the day" legality check.

## 10.

From `kb/priors.json`, `perfection_priors.json`, `calibration.json` (all exact, all enumerable):

- **Only 8 figures can be Judge** and they are exactly equiprobable: 8,192 casts each. The Judge
  carries 3 bits; a Mother carries 4. The court is structurally compressed.
- Of those 8, ill-complexioned figures are **25%** of outcomes; among all 16 they'd be 37.5% — the
  mechanism flatters the answer. (Label-dependent, so the number is only as good as the quality table
  in `figures.yaml`; al-Zanātī's own counts are 5 happy + 3 strong-happy + 4 distressful + 5 mixed.)
- **Perfection base rates**, e.g. a house-VII (marriage/partner) question: no relation 49.3%,
  translation 18.8%, conjunction 21.2%, **mutation 4.5%**, occupation 6.2%. So `occupation` (6.2%,
  = exactly 1/16 as the algebra demands) is real news; `translation` is nearly routine and by itself
  should not be read as "the stars say yes with help".
- Only **house XV** has restricted attainment; every other house can hold all 16 figures — so
  "figure X in house Y" is *not* intrinsically rare; rarity lives in the **combinations**.
- The 16 figures can never all be distinct in one cast; at least one repeats, always (0 counterexamples).

## 11.

1. **Puer vs Puella pattern.** Britannica's plate and the Golden Dawn table: Puer = 1-2-1-1,
   Puella = 1-1-2-1. Digital Ambler/Skinner line: the reverse. Everything else about the 16 agrees.
   Consequence: if you follow the other convention, **only these two labels swap** — the dots, the
   arithmetic and the derivations are untouched. The engine marks both names with `*`.
2. **Which planet rules which figure.** The Calatarama's f.5 gives Mars→Rubeus+Puella, Venus→Puer+Amissio,
   Jupiter→Acquisitio+Laetitia+Caput, Saturn→Tristitia+Carcer+Cauda, Mercury→Conjunctio+Populus,
   Moon→Albus+Via, Sun→Fortuna Major+Minor. The modern table differs for Puer/Puella/Populus/Via and
   for the Dragon's tail/head. This matters twice over: it decides the day/hour legality gate *and*
   the pace rule. The engine uses the Calatarama table for the gate (because it is that text's own
   table) and says so.

## 12.

Cattan's book III gives, per figure and per house, not a mood but an event. Two examples, from the
1591 scan I OCR'd myself (raw OCR string first, my reading of it second — **verify on the page image**):

```
p140  "Cbis figure P»er tntbis boufe, tbe man (ball be flaine, ft ^ is ill fo^tbe fiche pecfon,
        fo? ft figntfpetb Death"
   -> "This figure Puer in this house, the man shall be slain; it is ill for the sick person,
        for it signifieth death."

p140  "JMmg this figure %ubew in tines eight boufe, tbeman is ojthall be fiaiwe ioptb a füiojDeoj
        a ftaffe, the fiche perfsn is in Danger to Die"
   -> "If this figure Rubeus in the tenth [recte eighth] house, the man shall be slain with a
        sword or a staff; the sick person is in danger to die."
```

That is the class of statement you were asking about: a weapon, a body, a house, a verdict for the
sick man. `kb/cattan1591_dangers.json` is my page-index to 23 of these passages (topic, figure, page,
raw string) so they can be read off the scans properly. It is deliberately a **pointer**, not a
lookup table: at ~65% OCR recall on blackletter, a machine-extracted table would lie to you.
The clean path to a usable register is HTR (Transkribus/Kraken trained on 16th-c. secretary type) or
the 1578/1608 editions collated line by line — that is roadmap item 1.

## 13.

- The **"hidden figure"** of a house (used in Islamicate practice to get at "the secret and hidden root"
  by combining the house's figure with it) — the technique is *used* in the literature I can read,
  but I found no generation rule. Likely in al-Zanātī/Ṭarsīs material. Not invented.
- **Suffrages** and the geomantic **arrows** — named in the Latin tradition; need the Latin editions
  (Tannery II; *Estimaverunt Indi*) rather than the 1638 print I have, whose text layer is thin.
- Any **Ifá-derived** concrete danger/taboo. The structural map (16 figures ↔ 16 odu) is real and
  documented; the semantic bridge is not, and the author who publishes the map explicitly warns
  against assuming the oracles behave alike. Building a "beware of the knife" layer would mean either
  fabricating or importing Ifá's own *ewe/ebo* register — which is roadmap item 3, done properly.

# Second dig: the casebook stratum (added in this pass)

## 14.

Found in **Fasciculus geomanticus (1704)**, which prints *Alfagini Quaestiones Geomantici* — a
per-question rulebook. 42 rules OCR'd by me from the scan (`corpus/raw/ia/fasciculus_ocr/`,
`kb/alfagini_quaestiones.json`); the mechanisms, all now computed by `engine/questions.py`:

- **Turning the chart from the Judge**: 'for journeys at greater distance, reduce to the **third house
  from the Judge**' (leaf 460) — the Judge becomes an ascendant, giving a free second chart. Reported
  in both the 15-house frame (what the book means) and the 16-house one (what your chart has).
- **Figura extracta**: 'it will also be known from the figure drawn from IV and V' (leaf 465) — add two
  houses to get a *new* figure that names the hidden root. This is the answer to your 'something only a
  genius would know': it is a rule, not a gift.
- **Motion as verdict**: entering (intrans) keeps/recovers, leaving (exiens) loses — so *the same good
  figure* is 'gained and kept' or 'gained then departing' depending on its motion (leaf 463-465, 528).
- **Fixity as duration**: 'figures firm and fixed in VII signify fixed love; mobile ones inconstant;
  bicoreal ones mediocre' (leaf 480). A durability test, not a good/bad test.
- **Consent of the four angles** as the strength multiplier (leaf 463-465): 3-4 good angles = the
  answer will hold; none = it won't.
- **Forbidden-figure test in sickness**: 'see that in I, VIII and the **twelfth** no figure of Mars or of
  the Moon falls; if a figure of life is in I and the *same* passes into VIII, safety; if it does not
  pass, expect death' (leaf 474). My first reading had this as X and XI — *decimam secundam* is the
  12th; corrected, and the passage test is implemented.
- **Dreams by gender**: I to the sleeper, IX to the dream, III/VI/XI/XV to the thing asked;
  'masculine figures signify truth, feminine falsehood' (leaf 519).
- **Weather by element**: the figure most frequent in I and X gives the season's quality — fire =
  health, air = wind, water = corruption of the air and beasts, earth = cold and drought (leaf 521).
- **Political readings**: X to the king and realm, XIII to royal servants, I to courtiers, II to the
  people (leaf 525); 'give I to one army and VII to the other and see where the bad figures are' (498).
- **Servants, gifts, prison**: I & XI for fidelity (531); I & II/V/XI for a gift (531); and for
  imprisonment, whether the figure of I and XIII sits *in* XII or conjoined with it, i.e. in X or XI (532).

## 15.

Hartmann, *The Principles of Astrological Geomancy* (1889), has an appendix of **2,048 answers to 16
questions** — a lookup grid, 8 attainable Judges x 16 figures x 16 questions. 859 cells recovered into
`kb/hartmann_casebook.json`, plus his composition rules, which independently confirm the parentage
doctrine found in the Calatarama: 'A good figure made of two good ones is good; a bad of two bad ones
is bad; a good made of one good and one bad means success, but delay and vexation'; and
'if the two witnesses are good and the judge bad, the result will be obtained, but it will be
unfortunate in the end'.

The warning: Hartmann's **casting rule is not the shield**. He totals the punctuation points, divides
by 12, and the remainder *is* the Judge's house ('there have been 152 points... 8 is therefore in this
case the Judge'). So it is a different art wearing the same names — read his appendix as a
question-oracle keyed to figures, and never as a judgment of a shield chart.

## 16.

The Indian horary literature (Prasna Marga; the *Shatpanchashika* = 'sixty verses' on questions) is the
most systematic **sub-question inventory** in print: theft ('nature of the article; the thief — his age,
caste, etc.; direction and present location of the stolen article; whether recovered'), the absent
person, return with safety or profit, rain, the expected child's sex, whether the question concerns
self/brother/son/mother/enemy/wife/boss/father. Its shape is now recorded in `kb/question_inventory.json`
as `prasna_question_taxonomy`, and the slot table says which geomantic attribute answers each kind.
The content is **not** borrowed: those answers come from lunar mansions and planetary combinations.
Pasting a horary 'combination' onto a figure is how syncretic nonsense gets written.

## 17.

The **Amazulu bones divination** corpus (Colenso & Bryant, 1870, 195k words, in `corpus/raw/ia2/`) shows
the mechanism plainly: each thrown lot has a *name that is itself a statement* — an informant, a lurking
enemy, a sickness in the house, a warning in a dream. The diviner reads **named particulars**, not
adjectives. That, plus the figura extracta, plus the Calatarama's who/where table, is the honest route to
'a visitor is coming' statements: they are *vocabulary*, held by a position, not prophecy. Ifá's *ewe*
(taboo) and *ebo* (prescription) registers do the same job with a different cosmology — and access to
that register runs through a teacher, not a download. See docs/RESEARCH_LEDGER.md §3.

## 18.

- Most items titled *Ramal*/*Raml* in South Asia are **not** figure geomancy: the Hindi
  'Ramal Prashnottari'/'Ramal Sara' books are the Napoleon-**Book-of-Fate** lookup genre (Voltaire's
  *Zadig* ancestry), number-and-table, no figures at all. Real geomancy in that region is the
  *ilm-i raml* of the Persian/Arabic MSS (SurkhAb, Nuskhah-i raml), which I have as text layers but
  which need Arabic/Persian OCR work before any rule can be lifted.
- The IA copy of Skinner's *Terrestrial Astrology* is an unofficial upload. It is in the catalogue so
  you know it exists; do not build a corpus on it.


## 19.

From IA I pulled **Jean de la Taille, *La Geomancie*** (`corpus/raw/ia3/lataille_la_geomancie.txt`;
8 house-IV rulings extracted into `kb/lataille_grid.json` — the abridged print loses most section
headings, so coverage is honestly partial). Three of its rules are new to our stack and are now in
`kb/techniques.yaml`:

1. **The mobile/communal tie-break** — *"si les figures estoient mobiles ou communes, preferez le
   jugement selon les qualitez des maisons."* Every modern book falls silent when the figure is
   ambiguous; la Taille says: then **the house decides**. `engine/questions.py` now implements it
   (angle = upheld, succedent = held slowly, cadent = falls away) and a test proves it fires *only*
   when the figure is mobile or communal (50 firings across the registered rules).
2. **The ending is read separately** — *"la quatriesme avec la quinziesme la fin"*: the 4th with the
   Judge tells how the matter **ends**, which is not the same question as whether it succeeds.
3. **Guarded hidden things** — Populus in the 4th: *"le thresor cache est en la garde du Diable"* —
   the thing is not lost but shielded; recovery needs a deliberate act or a third party.

And a fourth, uncomfortable but important: his attribution table disagrees with everyone else's
(Amissio/*Perte* = "Venus retrograde, in Scorpio, of the element of Fire"). That is recorded as
`figure_attribution_variants`, with the instruction: **re-run** any element/planet rule under the
alternative table before stating it as a finding. Averaging the tables is how a synthesis becomes fiction.

## 21.

Hartmann's `2,048 Answers` table is addressed **8 attainable Judges x 16 cofigures x 16 questions**,
not 16 x 16 x 8 (verified against the extracted rows on 2026-09-17: `q` runs 1..16, and only
even-pointed figures ever appear as judge). Two consequences:

* **Independent corroboration.** Every judge figure in the 859 cells we have transcribed is even in
  points and is one of the 8 figures the shield can produce as Judge - the same set our exhaustive
  sweep over all 65,536 casts derives from the arithmetic alone. A 2,048-cell printed table from 1889
  agreeing with a theorem proved by enumeration is the strongest external check in this library.
* **A measurable hole.** `library/tools/check_hartmann_grid.py` prints per-judge coverage
  (Carcer: 0/256 - the whole block is missing, hence 41.9% and not scattered gaps), and
  `kb/hartmann_casebook.json` `_meta.next_extraction` names the biggest holes to OCR first.
  The blocker is identified, not vague: IA's text layer for `b24884145` ends before the appendix
  (173 KB, 2 mentions of Carcer), so only page-level OCR of the scans can finish it.

## 22.

`kb/calatarama_grid.json` carries <!--num:grid_cells-->**184 of a possible <!--num:grid_possible-->**192<!--/num--> figure×house cells across **all twelve
houses**. It used to carry 142 over ten, and the difference was not the manuscript: it was three
defects in `scripts/extract_calatarama_grid.py`, each one of which made a tooling failure look like a
historical silence.

1. **The alias map omitted the canonical name `Carcer`.** The tables label that figure `[Carcer]` in
   eleven places; the map had `Carçel` and `Carcel` but not `Carcer`, so one row vanished from every
   table and the figure that governs detention, prison and death appeared to be judged nowhere.
2. **A strict header pattern skipped two whole tables.** Eleven universal tables are headed
   `Meaning of the Geomantic Figures in the N House (f. ..)`, but house VIII reads *"...geomantic
   **house** in the VIII House"* and house X drops the article. Both tables exist, complete, at f.50
   and f.56v; both were simply never opened.
3. **Sections were cut by headings quoted in commentary**, and the last table ran unbounded into
   Appendix 2, so house XII's `Via` cell ended up holding 600 characters of the transcriber's note on
   editorial brackets instead of a judgement.

Repairing these also corrected a **misattribution that was already published**: the v0.2.0 grid filed
house X's `Laetitia` row under house IX. The rows in these tables follow a fixed order, so an
off-by-one in section slicing moves a judgement to the wrong house silently; the offsets now prove
each cell sits inside its own table.

Two cells remain empty and both are **the source's own omissions**, recorded in
`_meta.editorial_notes` rather than papered over:

- **house XII / Populus** — the table prints `[Populo]` with no text and footnote 706 says
  *"No information is given for this figure."* A lacuna with a citation is a finding.
- **house VIII / Acquisitio** — the table has sixteen rows but labels `Puela` twice; the row whose
  position is Acquisitio's reads *"Paying debts, acquiring an inheritance and the sick person, if
  danger."* The content and the row order of the other ten tables both point at Acquisitio, but the
  label does not, so the cell stays empty until Barcelona MS 84.7.4 f.50 is re-read. Guessing here
  would put words in a 13th-century hand.

House I has **no universal table by design**: the manual presents the first house through the example
casting (Finan's Table 8, f.24v-25), and those ten rows are cited from there.

The structural lesson is in the code, not just here: the parser now emits
`_meta.unmatched_labels` (any bracketed label it could not resolve — empty means every row was
claimed), and `engine/retrieve.py --build` writes `missing_figure_rulings` into every house row and
`missing_house_rulings` into every figure row, so an app can render "this table does not speak to
this" instead of showing a silently shorter list. Coverage is asserted against the data by
`library/tools/check_calatarama_grid.py`, which fails when a figure is missing from *every* house —
the signature of a parser bug rather than a source gap.

## 23. Astrology, signs, and translation

The library already computed *translation* as a mode of perfection (a third figure
beside both significators). It did not yet say how **zodiac signs** and **planets**
colour that third party. That gap is closed in `kb/astrology.yaml`, without inventing
a rule.

- **Translation is geomantic, not horary.** The test is identity of figures in
  neighbouring houses. It is named after astrology's translation of light because
  the *job* is a go-between, not because applying aspects of planets are counted.
- **What astrology then adds:** the translator's planet (who the helper is), the
  translator's sign (Agrippa 1655 table vs Calatarama planets — they disagree, both
  ship), house aspect when perfection is denied (Cattan; d'Abano), and company
  demi-simple (same planet, different figure — teaching-book, MED).
- **Agrippa's house chart** (Mothers on the angles in solar order, cadents rebuilt)
  is recorded as `NAMED_NOT_DEFAULT`. The Shield allotment remains the engine.
- **Gerard of Cremona / astronomical geomancy** stays `NAMED_ONLY` (FINDINGS s.11);
  still no generative rule in hand.
- Dexter vs sinister aspect direction is named in modern manuals and **not**
  computed until a folio states the counting.

See `notes/ASTROLOGY_AND_TRANSLATION.md`.
