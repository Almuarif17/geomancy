# Ideas and preferences from the owner — the input channel

This file exists so that "I have an idea" has somewhere to land that is not a chat window. Write in plain
words; do not worry about format, spelling, or whether it is technical. Each entry becomes one of three
things: a **preference** (`app/preferences.json`), a **work item** (the pipeline itself), or a **decline with
a reason** (recorded, so it is not re-litigated). Nothing is silently dropped: a decision you cannot see the
reason for is how a project stops being yours.

Write a new `### I` block at the bottom. The cheapest useful form is one sentence about what you wanted to
happen and what actually happened.

## Open questions, in the order they would change the most

### Q1 — Which authors do you actually trust, and why?
`app/preferences.json` → `preferred_works_first` decides who gets quoted first on every claim. Right now it
says Calatarama, Cattan 1608, Hartmann, because that is the order of *completeness*, not the order of your
judgement. If your instinct is that Cattan should lead because he is plainer, or that the Arabic-layer sources
should lead because the tradition came through them, say so: it changes the reading on every page, and it is
yours to decide, not the data's.

### Q2 — Which questions do people actually ask you?
22 outcomes are wired. `topics_pinned` is cosmetic; the real move is telling me which of these you have never
been asked, and which question you get weekly that is not in the list at all. Breadth here is the difference
between "a geomancy dataset" and "the geomancy dataset someone's app can depend on".

### Q3 — Whose wording do you want on the page?
The library currently shows what sources said, in their English (16th-to-21st century). A plain-modern
paraphrase layer is a *product decision*, and it is the biggest thing missing for a paying customer. Say
which you prefer for a first draft of a reading: source wording, modern wording, or both side by side.

### Q4 — What should make you wince?
Tell me about a reading in the app that embarrassed you — a claim that looked like a prediction, a house that
seemed wrong, a gap that should not have been a gap. Those become gates, and gates are the only reason the
next thousand rows are as careful as the first.
